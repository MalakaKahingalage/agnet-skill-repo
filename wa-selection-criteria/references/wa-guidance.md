## Purpose

Use this reference to write a strong, concise, evidence-based response to WA Government selection criteria and work-related requirements.

This guidance is designed for roles advertised through WA public sector channels and is grounded in:
- Jobs and Skills WA guidance on responding to selection criteria
- WA public sector guidance on merit-based recruitment
- WA Public Sector Commission capability and leadership frameworks

## Core Principle

A winning response does not say:
- `I believe I have strong skills...`
- `I am confident I can...`

A winning response proves:
- what the candidate did
- how they did it
- why it mattered
- what result they achieved

Panels are looking for evidence, not intent.

## What WA Panels Assess

WA public sector recruitment is merit-based. Written applications are used to assess whether the candidate meets the role's work-related requirements.

Panels commonly assess:
- skills and abilities
- experience
- qualifications and training
- work performance
- capacity to achieve required outcomes
- relevant personal qualities
- potential for further development

Practical implication:
1. show a match to the requirement
2. support it with evidence from a real example
3. demonstrate a result at the level expected for the role

## The Single Best Structure: Mini-STAR

For WA roles, use STAR or SAO. The most effective version is a compressed STAR.

Recommended response shape:
- 1 sentence direct claim
- 1-2 sentences situation or task context
- 3-5 sentences action
- 1-2 sentences result and value
- 1 sentence tie-back to the role

Weighting rule:
- situation or task: 20-25%
- action: 55-65%
- result: 15-20%

If the example spends too long on background, it weakens the answer.

## Gold-Standard Formula For Each Criterion

Use this pattern:

`Criterion heading`

`Claim`
I have demonstrated [criterion wording] through [brief context].

`Example`
In my role as [role], I was responsible for [task or problem]. The key challenge was [complexity or stakes]. I [specific action]. I [specific action]. I [specific action], including [tools, stakeholders, or process].

`Result`
As a result, [measurable outcome, service improvement, risk reduction, or stakeholder outcome].

`Tie-back`
This demonstrates my ability to [criterion wording] in a WA Government environment.

## The Ideal Writing Style

Do:
- write in first person: `I led`, `I analysed`, `I resolved`
- use the same keywords as the JDF or advert
- show scale, complexity, stakeholders, judgement, and outcomes
- quantify where possible: time, cost, volume, risk, service, compliance, adoption
- choose examples from the last 2-5 years where possible
- use examples at the same or higher level than the target role

Do not:
- speak in theory
- use generic claims with no proof
- overuse `we` without clarifying the candidate's role
- paste the same example into multiple criteria without tailoring it
- write long autobiographical background
- ignore page limits or formatting instructions

## How To Pick The Right Example

Choose examples that are:
1. relevant to the criterion
2. recent enough to feel current
3. complex enough for the role level
4. clearly the candidate's own work
5. rich in outcomes

Strong example types:
- solved a difficult problem
- improved a process
- handled stakeholders with competing needs
- delivered under pressure or tight deadlines
- managed risk, compliance, safety, or probity
- influenced a decision using evidence
- led change, uplift, training, or service improvement

## How To Decode A Criterion Properly

Many WA criteria contain multiple parts.

Example:
`Well-developed communication and interpersonal skills with the ability to liaise effectively with internal and external stakeholders.`

Break it into components:
- communication skills
- interpersonal skills
- liaison ability
- internal stakeholders
- external stakeholders
- effectiveness

The response should hit all parts, not just one.

Fast method:
- underline verbs such as `liaise`, `analyse`, `deliver`, `manage`, `lead`
- identify quality words such as `effective`, `high-level`, `sound`, `well-developed`
- identify context words such as `stakeholders`, `policy`, `service delivery`, `compliance`, `complex environment`
- build the example to match all of them

## How To Tailor To WA Public Sector Expectations

Where relevant, show evidence of:
- public service professionalism
- integrity and probity
- client or community focus
- evidence-based judgement
- collaboration
- diversity and inclusion awareness
- adaptability and learning
- delivery against priorities

These themes align strongly with WA capability and leadership frameworks.

## WA Capability Alignment Cheat Sheet

The WA Public Sector Commission capability profile for Levels 1-6 uses five core clusters:
1. Shapes and manages strategy
2. Achieves results
3. Builds productive relationships
4. Exemplifies personal integrity and self-awareness
5. Communicates and influences effectively

Use this in practice:
- planning, judgement, analysis -> `Shapes and manages strategy`
- delivery, execution, improvement -> `Achieves results`
- stakeholder management, teamwork -> `Builds productive relationships`
- ethics, resilience, accountability -> `Exemplifies personal integrity and self-awareness`
- writing, presenting, influencing -> `Communicates and influences effectively`

If applying for senior roles, also align examples to WA leadership expectations, especially:
- lead collectively
- think through complexity
- dynamically sense the environment
- deliver on high leverage areas
- build capability
- embody the spirit of public service
- lead adaptively

## Length Guidance

Always follow the advert or application package first.

If no clear limit is given, a practical WA guide suggests:
- about half a page to one page per criterion depending on complexity
- concise responses are usually stronger than long unfocused ones

Best practical target:
- 150-300 words for straightforward criteria
- 250-400 words for complex or senior criteria

Cut anything that does not strengthen evidence.

## The Strongest Response Pattern

Example criterion:
`Builds productive relationships and liaises effectively with stakeholders`

Example response:

I have strong experience building productive relationships and liaising effectively with internal and external stakeholders in operational and project environments. In my previous role, I was responsible for coordinating a cross-functional service improvement initiative involving operations, vendors, and business leaders with competing priorities. I established a stakeholder map, clarified decision rights, scheduled targeted working sessions, and created concise status updates that highlighted risks, dependencies, and required actions. Where issues emerged, I engaged directly with the relevant stakeholders, reframed technical issues in business terms, and negotiated practical next steps to maintain momentum. As a result, the initiative was delivered on schedule, stakeholder responsiveness improved, and several process bottlenecks were resolved without escalation. This demonstrates my ability to build productive relationships and communicate effectively across diverse stakeholder groups.

Why this works:
- direct claim
- specific context
- mostly action
- clear outcome
- explicit tie-back

## Weak Vs Strong Phrasing

Weak:
- `I am an excellent communicator.`
- `I always work well with others.`
- `I have extensive experience in stakeholder engagement.`
- `We completed the project successfully.`

Strong:
- `I prepared executive-ready updates that translated technical risks into business impacts and decisions.`
- `I built trust with internal and external stakeholders by clarifying priorities, following through on actions, and resolving issues early.`
- `I engaged regulators, vendors, and internal leaders to align scope, timelines, and compliance requirements.`
- `I led the remediation plan, which reduced turnaround time by 30% and removed a recurring service risk.`

## The Best Editing Checklist

Before submitting, confirm:
1. every part of the criterion is answered
2. at least one real example is used
3. the response is mostly about the candidate's actions
4. `I` is used clearly enough
5. an outcome or result is included
6. the language matches the JDF
7. the example is at the right level for the role
8. filler and generic claims are removed
9. page, word, and formatting rules are followed exactly
10. a stranger could understand the candidate's value without extra context
