# WA Selection Criteria

## Overview

Use this skill to draft or improve WA Government selection criteria responses that are concise, evidence-based, and explicitly matched to the advertised work-related requirements.

Prioritize proof over claims. Show what the candidate did, how they did it, and what outcome they achieved.

## Core Workflow

1. Read the criterion and break it into its component parts.
   Extract:
   - verbs such as `liaise`, `analyse`, `deliver`, `lead`, `manage`
   - quality markers such as `effective`, `sound`, `well-developed`, `high-level`
   - context markers such as `stakeholders`, `policy`, `service delivery`, `compliance`, `complex environment`

2. Match the criterion to the user's strongest example.
   Prefer examples that are:
   - clearly attributable to the user
   - recent
   - relevant to the role level
   - rich in outcomes, scale, complexity, or stakeholder management

3. Draft in compressed STAR form.
   Use:
   - 1 sentence direct claim
   - 1-2 sentences situation/task
   - 3-5 sentences action
   - 1-2 sentences result
   - 1 sentence tie-back to the criterion

4. Keep most of the response on action.
   Trim long background sections and generic self-descriptions.

5. Reuse the job ad's language naturally.
   Mirror the criterion wording where it improves fit, but do not copy the ad verbatim in a robotic way.

6. Align to WA public sector expectations where relevant.
   Show evidence of judgement, integrity, collaboration, service focus, adaptability, and delivery against priorities.

7. End with a clear tie-back.
   Make it obvious how the example demonstrates the exact requirement in a WA public sector setting.

## Writing Rules

- Write in first person: `I led`, `I analysed`, `I resolved`.
- Prefer evidence over opinion or intent.
- Quantify where possible: time, turnaround, cost, risk, volume, service improvement, compliance, adoption.
- Clarify the user's individual contribution when the example involves a team.
- Prefer examples from the last 2-5 years when possible.
- Choose examples that match the seniority and complexity of the target role.
- Remove filler, theory, autobiography, and repeated phrasing.

Do not write lines such as:
- `I believe I have strong skills...`
- `I am confident I can...`
- `I always work well with others...`

## Review Checklist

Before finalizing, confirm:
- every part of the criterion has been addressed
- the response uses a real example
- the action section carries most of the weight
- the user's role is explicit
- the response includes a meaningful result
- wording aligns with the JDF or advert
- the example is at the right level for the role
- length and formatting match the application instructions
